# Eczema Analysis Session - 11 April 2026

Interactive analysis of ~2.5 weeks of tracking data (Mar 23 - Apr 11), covering 136 meals, 38 skin checks, 33 medication entries, and 23 notes/events.

## 1. Timeline & Phases

### Phase 1: Urea-aggravated period (Mar 23 - Apr 1) — severity 7-9

Had been using CeraVe cream with 5% urea for ~2 months, with skin worsening throughout. Urea is a known irritant on broken eczematous skin (~10% "toxic" reaction rate in studies). Steroids (Eleuphrat, Sudocrem, Elidel) applied regularly but didn't bring severity down — though this likely reflects the ongoing urea irritation overwhelming the steroids, not steroid ineffectiveness per se. GP visit on Apr 1 prescribed additional Diprosone OV. Heavy steroid application Apr 1-2.

Stress was also present during this phase ("Stressful day" noted Mar 24 and Mar 30). The stress-eczema connection is well-established: stress impairs skin barrier function and shifts immunity toward allergic responses. This was likely compounding the urea irritation.

### Phase 2: Improvement (Apr 2-4) — severity 9 -> 3

Multiple things changed simultaneously:
- **Stopped urea moisturizer** (noted Mar 31) — removed an active irritant
- **Heavy steroid push** Apr 1-2 — Diprosone OV + Eleuphrat + QV cream
- **Ocean snorkeling** on Apr 4 — 1hr in 22°C ocean with wetsuit, cold outdoor shower after

The severity trajectory was: 9 (Apr 2) -> 6 (Apr 4 AM) -> 5 (Apr 4 PM) -> **3** (Apr 5 AM, best reading in dataset).

The sev=3 appears to be a step change associated with the snorkeling, not a gradual trend from stopping urea — the sharp V-shape (down to 3, then immediately back up after Apr 5 food) suggests something acute. If it were purely urea removal, the recovery would be monotonic.

### Phase 3: Rebound flare (Apr 5-6) — severity 3 -> 8

After the sev=3 morning, had a large Papparich lunch (~14:00 local: shrimp, wat tan hor, char kuai teow, cashew stir fry, shrimp dumplings) followed by Frutips blackcurrant pastilles (~17:16). Spots noticed by 17:00 (sev=5), full flare by 21:00 (sev=7). Italian wedding soup (with bay leaves) eaten ~21:05 on Apr 6, sev=8 check at ~21:34. Next day sev=8.

**Important caveat on food reaction timing:** Eczema food reactions come in two types. Immediate IgE-mediated reactions occur within 2 hours. But delayed non-IgE-mediated reactions — which are the more common type in eczema — take **6-48 hours** to manifest. Clinical food challenges use a 2-day observation window. This means:
- The Apr 5 flare could plausibly be from food eaten on **Apr 3-4** (Shin Ramyun kimchi, beef curry, bakery tarts, canned tuna), not just the same-day Papparich/frutips
- The 29-minute gap between the bay leaf soup and the sev=8 check is consistent with IgE-mediated timing, but there's a 14-hour gap since the previous check (sev=6 that morning), so the flare could have started before the soup
- The user was also unsure whether worsening had started before the Papparich meal

### Phase 4: New baseline (Apr 7-11) — severity 5-7

Severity oscillating 5-7. This appears to be the true baseline post-urea. Back slowly healing (browning/drying out), but new spots appearing on shoulder, arm, thighs. This phase has a high dietary histamine load (see Finding 5 below), which may be maintaining the elevated baseline.

## 2. Key Findings

### Finding 1: Urea moisturizer was an active irritant (confirmed)
Stopping CeraVe 5% urea was necessary for improvement. Had been using it for ~2 months with consistent worsening. Severity was 8-9 while using it. Urea irritation on broken eczematous skin is documented in literature — it acts as a "toxic" irritant due to hypertonicity and acidity, particularly at higher concentrations and on compromised skin barrier.

### Finding 2: Snorkeling produced a step-change improvement (strong signal, needs replication)
The strongest acute improvement in the dataset: severity went from 5 -> 3 overnight (best ever reading), a sharp V-shape, not a gradual decline.

**Possible mechanisms (not yet separated):**
- **Saltwater**: antimicrobial, osmotic exfoliation, mineral content. Dead Sea balneotherapy is evidence-based for eczema — studies show improved skin barrier function, hydration, and reduced inflammation. However, Dead Sea mineral composition (high Mg, K, Br) differs significantly from regular ocean water, so the mechanism may not be identical.
- **Sweat-free exercise**: unlike gym/running, sweat is immediately washed away in water. Sweat is one of the most commonly reported eczema exacerbants — sweat cytokines (IL-1, IL-31) directly activate keratinocytes and trigger inflammation. This may be why snorkeling helped while gym/running shows slight next-day worsening.
- **Cold water**: cold outdoor shower + ocean temp. Anti-inflammatory effect.
- **Wetsuit**: physical exfoliation. Skin was visibly flaky afterwards.
- **Breaking the itch-scratch-sleep feedback loop**: woke at 05:45, long active day, likely slept deeply. The sleep-eczema relationship is bidirectional (r=0.70-0.72 between severity and scratching episodes). Worse skin -> more itching -> worse sleep -> more scratching -> worse skin. Exhaustion may have broken this cycle for one night.
- **Stress relief / mental relaxation** from a day out.

This was a single event — needs replication to confirm.

### Finding 3: Steroids were overwhelmed by the urea irritant (inconclusive on steroid effectiveness)
Steroid days during Phase 1 showed no improvement over non-steroid days (both 8-9). However, this doesn't mean steroids are ineffective — they were fighting a losing battle against an ongoing irritant. We don't have good data on steroid effectiveness *without* the urea confound, since steroids were mostly stopped after urea was stopped. Needs discussion with dermatologist.

### Finding 4: Sleep and skin severity are a feedback loop (well-supported, undertracked)
Two "poor sleep" notes (Mar 28, 29) both preceded severity 8+. The exhaustion-driven deep sleep on the snorkeling day may have contributed to improvement. The literature shows strong bidirectional correlation: AD patients spend up to 14.3% of sleep time scratching, and scratching is associated with sleep fragmentation and reduced sleep efficiency. Both directions matter: bad skin causes bad sleep AND bad sleep causes worse skin. Currently only 2 ad-hoc sleep notes in the data — needs systematic daily tracking.

### Finding 5: Dietary histamine load is high and likely relevant (strong hypothesis, untested)
The Phase 4 diet (Apr 6-11) is heavily loaded with high-histamine foods:
- Feta cherry tomato pasta with cottage cheese (daily, aging as leftovers — aged cheese + tomato + cottage cheese)
- Lao gan ma (fermented chili crisp)
- Shin Ramyun kimchi (fermented)
- Cuttlefish, canned tuna, salmon sushi (fish/seafood)
- Osteria Luna dinner Apr 8: tomato crab pasta + negroni (alcohol + shellfish + tomato — triple histamine hit, followed by new rash spots)
- Chocolate (frequent)

About 31% of AD patients show histamine intolerance. A subgroup has reduced diamine oxidase (DAO) activity, making them less able to break down dietary histamine. A low-histamine diet showed 33-100% efficacy in clinical trials.

**The leftover/batch-cooking pattern amplifies this.** The user eats batch-cooked meals over 5 days. Histamine accumulates in stored food even at refrigeration temperature (4-10°C) through bacterial amino acid decarboxylation. Crucially, **reheating does not destroy histamine** — it is heat-stable. Day-5 leftover pasta (already high-histamine from aged cheese + tomato) would have meaningfully more histamine than day-1. Leftovers are explicitly listed as a high-histamine food category in the clinical literature.

Current tracking does not capture food age — this is an important gap.

**On DAO testing:** A blood test for serum DAO activity exists, but has a high false positive rate (44% of the general population falls below "normal" cutoffs). The recommended diagnostic approach is empirical: try a low-histamine diet for 2-4 weeks and observe. DAO supplements (available OTC) can also be used as both treatment and diagnostic signal. Worth discussing with derm.

### Finding 6: Sweat/exercise aggravates skin (consistent with literature)
- Gym (Mar 26): same day sev=6, next day sev=7
- Running (Apr 9): no check, next day sev=6
- Parkrun (Apr 10, very sunny): same day sev=6, next day sev=7
- Post-run redness noted on Apr 11

Sweat is one of the most commonly reported eczema exacerbants. Sweat cytokines (IL-1, IL-31) directly activate keratinocytes and trigger inflammation. The consistent next-day severity uptick after land-based exercise is consistent with this. Snorkeling (also exercise) produced the opposite effect, likely because sweat is immediately washed away in water. **Implication: swimming/water exercise may be preferable to running/gym.**

### Finding 7: Stress contributes to the feedback loop (well-established, undertracked)
Two "stressful day" notes (Mar 24, Mar 30) both coincided with high severity. The user also noted "bad skin made me stressed" — confirming the bidirectional nature. Stress impairs skin barrier function and shifts immunity toward allergic responses via HPA axis disruption. Not enough data to quantify — needs consistent tracking.

### Finding 8: Telfast provides acute relief (observed, consistent with standard care)
On Apr 8, arm redness visibly reduced ~1hr after taking Telfast (antihistamine). Used 3 times (Apr 5, 6, 8), each during/after flares. Worth asking derm whether regular (vs reactive) antihistamine use would help maintain a lower baseline.

## 4. Experiments

### Experiment 1: Ocean swim (highest priority)
**Goal:** Replicate the snorkeling result.
**Protocol:** Go for an ocean swim or snorkel. Log skin before entry, immediately after, and next morning. Note water temperature and time in water.
**Variation:** Try salt baths at home (Dead Sea salt or magnesium-rich bath salts) as a cheaper test of the saltwater hypothesis. Note: Dead Sea mineral composition differs from regular ocean water, so this tests a related but not identical hypothesis.
**What we learn:** Whether saltwater exposure reliably produces a step-change improvement.

### Experiment 2: Low-histamine week (highest dietary priority)
**Goal:** See if baseline drops below 5 by reducing total histamine load.
**Protocol:** For 7+ days: no aged cheese, no fermented foods (kimchi, lao gan ma), no canned fish, avoid tomatoes, limit chocolate, no alcohol. Eat freshly cooked meals — if batch-cooking, freeze portions immediately and defrost-to-eat rather than keeping 5-day fridge leftovers. Log skin at least 2x/day.
**What we learn:** Whether histamine load is contributing to the 5-7 baseline. If baseline drops, reintroduce individual high-histamine items one at a time to identify the worst offenders.
**Variation:** If a full low-histamine week is too disruptive, start with: (a) stop the feta pasta, and (b) freeze leftover portions instead of fridging them for 5 days.

### Experiment 3: Track sleep quality daily
**Goal:** Build enough data to quantify the sleep-severity feedback loop.
**Protocol:** Rate sleep quality each morning (1-5 scale, hours, whether you scratched). A morning notification prompt would help.
**What we learn:** How strongly sleep quality predicts next-day severity, and vice versa. Informs whether sleep hygiene interventions could help.

### Experiment 4: Frutips re-challenge
**Goal:** Confirm or rule out frutips as a trigger.
**Protocol:** Wait for stable low severity (~3 days at sev <= 5). Eat frutips on an otherwise "safe" (low-histamine, freshly cooked) food day. Log skin at 1h, 3h, 6h, 12h, and 24h after — the extended window accounts for delayed non-IgE reactions.
**When:** Only when baseline is stable and not confounded by other changes.

## 5. Analysis Methodology — Problems & Next Steps

### Why the current analysis doesn't work

The existing analysis algorithm computes **lift of ingredients appearing before rising-edge flares**. Three fundamental problems:

1. **Rising-edge detection fails with sustained high severity.** Found only 4 "flares" from 38 checks because severity was consistently 7-9 for 10 days. Designed for occasional spikes against calm baseline, not chronic inflammation.

2. **Only 20% of meals have parsed ingredients.** 109 of 136 meals never had ingredient parsing run. The algorithm works with a tiny, non-representative fraction of the food data.

3. **Not enough data for statistical significance.** With 4 detected flares and 27 parsed meals, any correlation is noise. The "top suspects" (rice, pepper, garlic) are common ingredients appearing by coincidence.

### What might work better

**Short-term (current data):**
- **Elimination experiments** — most reliable with small datasets. This is how food triggers are actually diagnosed clinically (double-blind placebo-controlled food challenge with 2-day observation).
- **Narrative analysis** (what we did in this session) — reading the timeline using domain knowledge and qualitative notes.

**Medium-term (more data + app improvements):**
- **Day-level severity delta model:** Look at day-over-day severity changes. For each food/activity, compare mean severity delta on days consumed vs not. Simpler, no "flare" concept needed.
- **Lagged correlation:** Does eating X today predict higher severity tomorrow? Needs ~30+ days of complete data.
- **Confound-adjusted analysis:** Model severity as a function of food + sleep + exercise + stress + food age.

**Longer-term:**
- **Bayesian updating:** Start with priors from known eczema triggers, update with observed data. Works with small datasets.
- **N-of-1 trial design:** Structured crossover experiments (eat suspect food for 3 days, avoid for 3 days, repeat).

### Immediate data quality fixes

1. **Batch-parse the 109 unparsed meals** — without this, ingredient-level analysis is impossible.
2. **Set up ingredient aliases** — "Weet bix", "Weetbix", "Weet-Bix" should map to the same thing.
3. **Add sleep quality + stress tracking** — most impactful new data sources.
4. **Add food age/freshness tracking** — critical for histamine analysis.
5. **Increase skin check consistency** — 2 days had 0 checks (Apr 2, Apr 9). Aim for minimum 2/day (morning + evening).
