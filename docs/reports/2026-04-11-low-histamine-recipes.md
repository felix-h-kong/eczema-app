# Low-Histamine High-Protein Recipes

Asian-focused, meal-prep friendly. All recipes designed for: cook 5 portions on Sunday, eat 1 fresh, freeze 4, defrost one per day.

**No peanuts. No soy sauce** (use coconut aminos — made from coconut sap, not fermented like soy sauce). **No aged cheese, no tomato, no canned fish, no fermented condiments.**

**Protein target:** ~40-50g per serve for hypertrophy goals.

---

## The leafy green problem

Fresh Asian greens (bok choy, choy sum, kangkong) don't freeze well — they go soggy. Two approaches:

1. **Buy fresh greens midweek.** Grab a bunch of bok choy or choy sum on Wednesday — they keep 3-4 days in the fridge. Wilt into the reheated meal for 2 minutes in the microwave or a pan.
2. **Use freeze-friendly veg in the batch cook** (broccoli, broccolini, green beans, snap peas, carrots, zucchini) and save fresh greens for dinners when you cook fresh.

## Recipe 1: Hainanese-style chicken rice bowls

The foundation recipe. Simple, high protein, freezes perfectly.

**Makes 5 portions (~45g protein each)**

### Ingredients
- 1 kg chicken thigh (bone-in, skin-on for poaching; or 800g boneless thigh)
- 3 cups jasmine rice
- 4 cups chicken stock (fresh or frozen, not from a bouillon cube if avoiding MSG)
- 4 slices fresh ginger
- 3 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 stalks spring onion, whole
- Salt to taste

**Garlic ginger sauce (make a jar):**
- 4 tbsp fresh ginger, minced
- 3 tbsp garlic, minced
- 3 tbsp neutral oil (heated until shimmering, then poured over ginger/garlic)
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tbsp fresh lime juice

### Method
1. Poach chicken: bring stock + ginger + garlic + spring onion to boil. Add chicken, reduce to gentle simmer. Cook 25-30 min (bone-in) or 18-20 min (boneless) until 74°C internal.
2. Rest chicken in the liquid 10 min, then remove. Reserve poaching liquid.
3. Cook rice in rice cooker using poaching liquid instead of water (adds flavour).
4. Shred or slice chicken.
5. Make sauce: combine ginger + garlic in a bowl, pour hot oil over, stir in sesame oil + salt + lime juice.
6. Portion: chicken + rice into 5 containers/ziplocs. Sauce in a separate small container (or drizzle over before freezing — it's oil-based, freezes fine).
7. Eat 1 fresh with wilted bok choy. Freeze 4.

**When eating:** defrost, microwave, optionally wilt fresh greens into it.

## Recipe 2: Chicken fried rice

Adapted for low-histamine. Higher protein than typical fried rice by loading up the chicken and egg.

**Makes 5 portions (~42g protein each)**

### Ingredients
- 800g chicken breast, diced into 1cm cubes
- 8 eggs
- 4 cups cooked jasmine rice (cook fresh, spread on tray to cool slightly)
- 2 large carrots, finely diced
- 2 zucchini, finely diced
- 1 bunch spring onions, sliced (green parts)
- 4 tbsp coconut aminos
- 2 tbsp sesame oil
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- Salt and white pepper

### Method
1. Scramble eggs in batches with a little oil. Set aside, chop roughly.
2. Cook chicken in batches over high heat until browned. Set aside.
3. Stir-fry carrots and zucchini 2-3 min until just tender.
4. Add rice, toss over high heat 3-4 min.
5. Return chicken and egg, add coconut aminos and sesame oil, toss to combine.
6. Portion into 5 containers. Eat 1, freeze 4.

**When eating:** defrost, microwave. Good as-is. Optional: top with fresh coriander or a fried egg for extra protein.

## Recipe 3: Chicken congee (jook)

Freezes brilliantly. Comfort food. Very high protein if you load up the chicken. Naturally soupy, so reheats well — just add a splash of water.

**Makes 5 portions (~45g protein each)**

### Ingredients
- 800g chicken thigh (boneless, skin optional)
- 1.5 cups jasmine rice (rinsed)
- 2.5L water or fresh chicken stock
- 5 slices fresh ginger
- Salt to taste

**Toppings (add fresh when eating):**
- Spring onions, sliced
- Fresh coriander
- White pepper
- Sesame oil drizzle
- Fried shallots (optional — not fermented, just fried allium)

### Method
1. Bring stock/water + ginger to boil. Add whole chicken thighs and rice.
2. Reduce to low simmer, partially covered. Cook 1-1.5 hours, stirring occasionally, until rice has completely broken down into a thick porridge.
3. Remove chicken, shred with two forks, return to pot. Season with salt.
4. Portion into 5 containers. Eat 1 fresh with toppings. Freeze 4.

**When eating:** defrost, microwave, stir in a splash of hot water if too thick. Add fresh spring onion, coriander, white pepper, sesame oil on top.

**Protein boost:** crack a raw egg into the hot congee and stir — it cooks in ribbons like egg drop soup. Adds ~6g protein.

## Recipe 4: Salt and pepper chicken with rice

Crispy, simple, and the seasoning is literally just salt and pepper. Closest thing to takeaway that's also low-histamine.

**Makes 5 portions (~48g protein each)**

### Ingredients
- 1 kg chicken thigh (boneless, skin-on for crispiness)
- 3 cups jasmine rice
- 500g broccoli or broccolini, cut into florets
- 2 tbsp potato starch or cornstarch
- Salt and white pepper (generous)
- Neutral oil for pan-frying
- Fresh lime wedges for serving

### Method
1. Cook rice in rice cooker.
2. Cut chicken into bite-sized pieces. Toss with starch, salt, and white pepper.
3. Pan-fry in batches over medium-high heat, skin-side down first, until golden and crispy (4-5 min per side).
4. Steam or blanch broccoli/broccolini.
5. Portion: chicken + rice + broccoli into 5 containers. Eat 1, freeze 4.

**When eating:** defrost, microwave. Chicken won't be as crispy after freezing but still tastes good. Squeeze fresh lime over. Add fresh greens if you have them.

## Recipe 5: Chicken and vegetable rice noodle soup

Fast to assemble from frozen components. The trick: freeze the broth and chicken together, cook noodles fresh (they take 3 min).

**Makes 5 portions (~40g protein each)**

### Ingredients

**Batch cook and freeze:**
- 800g chicken thigh (boneless), sliced
- 2L fresh chicken stock
- 4 slices ginger
- 3 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 tbsp coconut aminos
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- Salt to taste

**Add fresh when eating:**
- 1 portion rice noodles (vermicelli or flat) per serve — cook in boiling water 3 min
- Fresh bok choy, choy sum, or baby spinach (NOT regular spinach — high histamine) — wilt in the hot broth
- Spring onion, fresh coriander
- White pepper
- Fresh lime squeeze

### Method
1. Simmer stock + ginger + garlic 10 min.
2. Add chicken, cook 8-10 min until done.
3. Season with coconut aminos, sesame oil, salt.
4. Portion broth + chicken into 5 containers. Eat 1 fresh, freeze 4.
5. When eating: defrost broth + chicken, heat in microwave or pot. Cook rice noodles fresh (3 min in boiling water). Wilt fresh greens in the hot broth. Pour over noodles.

## Quick reference: safe seasonings

| Use freely | Use in moderation | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Salt, white pepper, black pepper | Coconut aminos (soy sauce sub) | Soy sauce |
| Fresh garlic, fresh ginger | Sesame oil (small amounts) | Fish sauce |
| Fresh lime/lemon juice | Oyster sauce (lower histamine than soy) | Lao gan ma |
| Fresh herbs (coriander, basil, parsley, mint) | Curry powder (check for no added MSG) | Kimchi, miso |
| Spring onions / scallions | | Chili bean paste (doubanjiang) |
| Fresh chili (not fermented chili paste) | | Fermented shrimp paste |
| Garlic oil | | Vinegar (fermented) |

## Protein rotation

Chicken is the default in all recipes above, but swap freely to avoid monotony:

| Protein | Works in | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Chicken thigh/breast** | All 5 | The default. Thigh has more flavour, breast has more protein per gram. |
| **Beef** (fresh, e.g. chuck, rump, mince) | Rice bowls, fried rice, congee, noodle soup | Slice thin against the grain for stir-fry. Mince works great in congee and fried rice. |
| **Pork** (fresh, e.g. shoulder, loin, mince) | Congee, noodle soup, fried rice | You already like braised pork. Pork mince congee is a classic. |
| **Lamb** (fresh) | Rice bowls, fried rice | Great with cumin + garlic seasoning instead of ginger. |
| **Eggs** (as star protein) | Fried rice, congee, noodle soup | Use 3-4 eggs per serve to hit protein targets. Egg fried rice with extra egg is a legit meal. |
| **Firm tofu** (fresh, NOT fermented) | Rice bowls, fried rice, noodle soup | Pan-fry until golden for best texture. Press first to remove water. ~20g protein per 300g, so pair with egg to hit targets. |
| **Turkey** (fresh) | All 5 | Direct chicken substitute, leaner. Turkey mince in fried rice works well. |

**Avoid for batch cooking:** shellfish (histamine rises fast), canned/preserved meats, processed meats (bacon, sausage, deli).

**Fresh fish** is low-histamine but only if eaten same day — don't batch cook and freeze fish. Save it for fresh dinners.

### Example 2-week rotation
- Week 1: Beef fried rice
- Week 2: Chicken rice bowls with garlic ginger sauce
- Week 3: Pork congee (weekend comfort)
- Week 4: Salt and pepper chicken with rice
- Week 5: Lamb rice bowls with cumin and garlic

## Meal prep flow (Sunday)

1. Pick one recipe for the week
2. Cook
3. Portion into 5 ziploc bags or containers while still hot
4. Eat one fresh (with fresh greens if you have them)
5. Cool the rest briefly, then freeze flat
6. Each evening: move one portion from freezer to fridge
7. Midweek: grab fresh bok choy or choy sum from the shops if you want greens
