# Low-Histamine Meal Prep Plan

Designed for batch-cooking on Sunday, eating Mon-Fri. Based on consensus from multiple clinical studies on histamine intolerance and atopic dermatitis.

---

## What the research agrees on

### Avoid (unanimous across studies)
- Fermented foods: aged cheese, kimchi, soy sauce, fish sauce, lao gan ma, miso, sauerkraut
- Fermented beverages: wine, beer, kombucha
- Canned/preserved fish: tuna, sardines, mackerel, anchovy
- Alcohol (also inhibits DAO, the enzyme that breaks down histamine)

### Avoid (strong agreement)
- Tomato, spinach, eggplant, avocado (the only plant foods with measurably significant histamine levels)
- Chocolate / cocoa
- Aged or cured meats (salami, prosciutto, bacon)

### Safe staples
- **Fresh meat**: chicken, beef, lamb -- near-zero histamine when freshness is maintained
- **Grains**: rice, potato, sweet potato, oats, most breads (wheat is not a histamine issue, though it can be an allergen separately)
- **Most fresh vegetables**: broccoli, broccolini, bok choy, zucchini, carrots, green beans, capsicum, cauliflower, cucumber, lettuce, peas
- **Most fresh fruits**: apples, pears, blueberries, grapes, mango, watermelon, stone fruits
- **Fresh dairy**: fresh milk, butter, fresh cream (not aged/fermented)
- **Eggs**: low histamine (sometimes excluded from eczema diets for allergy reasons, not histamine)

### Contested / weak evidence
- Citrus fruits: 60% of low-histamine diets exclude them, but they contain no measurable histamine. Theory is they trigger histamine *release*, but no human studies support this. Keep or exclude based on personal observation.
- Strawberries, papaya: same story -- theoretical histamine liberators, limited evidence.
- Nuts: sometimes excluded, limited justification from histamine data specifically.

### Cooking method matters
- **Boiling / steaming / poaching** decreases histamine levels
- **Frying / grilling** increases them
- Roasting is somewhere in between -- acceptable but less ideal than boiling

## Sunday meal prep protocol

### Cook
- **Protein**: Fresh chicken thigh or breast. Poach, boil, or roast. Can also use fresh beef or lamb.
- **Base**: Rice (rice cooker) or boiled potatoes / sweet potato.
- **Vegetables**: Pick 2-3 from safe list. Broccoli, broccolini, bok choy, zucchini, carrots, green beans, capsicum, cauliflower, sweet potato.
- **Seasoning**: Salt, pepper, olive oil, fresh garlic, fresh ginger, fresh herbs (basil, parsley, coriander), fresh lemon juice (small amounts).

### Portion and freeze immediately
1. Portion into 5 ziploc bags or containers as soon as food is cool enough to handle
2. Eat one portion fresh (Sunday dinner)
3. Freeze the other 4 immediately -- **do not wait**. The goal is to freeze before bacteria start producing histamine.
4. Lay ziploc bags flat in the freezer for space efficiency and faster freeze/defrost.

### Daily
- Each evening: move tomorrow's lunch from freezer to fridge (overnight defrost)
- Reheat in microwave at lunch. Reheating doesn't remove histamine, but since you froze early, there's minimal accumulation.

## Swaps from current diet

| Current food | Why it's a problem | Swap |
|---|---|---|
| Feta cherry tomato pasta with cottage cheese | Aged cheese + tomato + 5-day fridge leftovers = triple histamine load increasing daily | Chicken rice bowl with fresh veg, portioned and frozen |
| Lao gan ma | Fermented chili condiment | Fresh chili, chili flakes, or fresh chili oil |
| Shin Ramyun kimchi | Fermented | Plain ramen or rice noodle soup with fresh veg and chicken |
| Canned tuna (John West, Wild Tides) | Canned fish is high histamine | Fresh chicken, or fresh fish eaten same day it's bought |
| Condensed milk in coffee | Processed, concentrated dairy | Fresh milk |
| Chocolate (daily) | Histamine trigger | Limit to occasional, not daily |
| Negroni / gin / alcohol | Histamine + inhibits DAO enzyme | Avoid during trial period |
| Soy sauce (in stir fries) | Fermented | Salt, fresh lemon/lime, oyster sauce (lower histamine) |

## What can stay
- Weet-Bix + fresh milk -- fine
- Fresh coffee with fresh milk -- fine
- Hot cross buns -- not a histamine issue (wheat/spice, not fermented). Keep for now.
- Roast chicken, beans, capsicum -- already a good low-histamine meal
- Fresh fruit (apples, pears, grapes, blueberries, mango, watermelon)
- Potato chips (fresh potato + oil + salt -- low histamine, though not nutritionally ideal)
- Croissant, bread + butter -- fine
- Black tea -- fine

## Example week

### Sunday
- **Cook**: Poached chicken thighs, rice, steamed broccolini + carrots + bok choy. Season with garlic, ginger, salt, olive oil.
- **Portion**: 5 bags. Eat 1, freeze 4.

### Monday-Friday
- **Breakfast**: Weet-Bix + fresh milk, or toast + butter, or eggs
- **Lunch**: Defrosted chicken rice bowl from Sunday cook
- **Snacks**: Fresh fruit, potato chips, hot cross bun, plain crackers
- **Dinner**: Keep simple and fresh -- eggs on toast, fresh stir-fry (no soy sauce, use salt + garlic), sandwich, or cook fresh

### Drinks
- Coffee with fresh milk
- Black tea
- Water
- Avoid: alcohol, chocolate drinks

## How to know if it's working

Run this for **2 weeks minimum** (clinical studies used 1-4 week trial periods). Log skin severity at least 2x/day (morning + evening) throughout.

**Success looks like**: baseline severity dropping below 5 consistently. The best indicator is your average severity over the second week compared to your current 5-7 baseline.

**If baseline drops**: reintroduce one high-histamine item at a time (e.g., add back tomato for 3 days, then cheese for 3 days) to identify the worst offenders. This way you don't have to stay on a restrictive diet permanently -- just avoid the specific things that affect you.

**If no change after 2 weeks**: histamine is probably not a major factor for you, and you can resume normal eating. Try investigating other suspects (bay leaves, allergy testing results, etc).
